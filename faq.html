Taxes & 0% CIT

0% if annual gross revenue stays under 14,000,000 ALL (≈€140,000) — set by Albanian Law No. 29/2023, running through 31 December 2029. Above that threshold, a flat 15% corporate income tax applies — still well below the EU average of ~21%.

VAT / IVA

Albania's standard VAT rate is 20% (6% for accommodation services, 0% for exports). VAT registration becomes mandatory once annual turnover exceeds 10,000,000 ALL (≈€100,000) — below that, registration is optional. Most small consulting or digital companies under the 0% CIT threshold also stay under the VAT threshold.

Ongoing Compliance

Yes — Albanian companies must keep proper accounting records and file annual (and often monthly) returns, even at 0% CIT. Our monthly retainers start from ~€100–200/month and cover bookkeeping, VAT declarations and annual statutory filing, so you stay compliant without tracking deadlines yourself.

Yes — every Albanian company must declare its ultimate beneficial owner(s) to the Beneficial Ownership Registry (RTEË) within statutory deadlines after registration, and update the filing whenever ownership changes. This is a standard EU-aligned AML requirement, not specific to foreign owners. We handle this filing as part of company formation and keep it updated as part of compliance retainers.

Each year: an annual financial statement filed with the NBC, an annual CIT return (even at 0%), and — if VAT-registered — ongoing monthly or quarterly VAT declarations. Beneficial ownership filings are updated only when ownership actually changes, not annually by default.

Late VAT or CIT filings typically incur fixed administrative penalties from the Albanian tax authority, plus interest on any unpaid tax. Penalties are modest compared to most EU jurisdictions but accumulate if ignored. This is exactly why we track every client's deadlines proactively under our monthly retainer rather than reactively.

Albania vs Other Countries

Albania's 0% CIT under €140K revenue beats Bulgaria's flat 10% and Romania's flat 16% (Romania has a restricted micro-enterprise regime that can lower this). Registration takes 3–5 days in Albania versus several weeks in both countries, and average labour costs in Albania are typically lower than in Romania.

North Macedonia and Serbia both apply a flat 10% corporate income tax with no equivalent to Albania's 0% small-company incentive. Albania additionally holds EU accession candidate status and a coastal location adjacent to Italy, which several clients cite as a logistical advantage for trade with Western Europe.

Malta's statutory rate is 35% (reduced to an effective ~5% via a refund mechanism, but this requires more complex structuring and ongoing compliance). Cyprus applies a flat 12.5% CIT. Both require deeper substance and higher running costs than Albania's straightforward 0%/15% structure, and registration in both typically takes longer than Albania's 3–5 days. Albania suits simpler structures better; Malta/Cyprus can suit specific holding-company or EU-passporting needs — we're happy to talk through which fits your situation.
