CORPORATE TAX IN ALBANIA

A 0% Corporate Tax Rate Is Real, and Here's Exactly How It Works.

Under Albanian Law No. 29/2023, companies with annual gross revenue below 14,000,000 ALL (roughly €151,000 at current rates) pay 0% corporate income tax. Not a reduced rate, not a temporary holiday tied to a special zone — a standing rule in the tax code, available to any qualifying company registered in Albania.

Company ownership doesn't affect eligibility. A UK-owned consultancy, a US-owned e-commerce business, or a Swiss-owned agency qualifies on exactly the same terms as an Albanian-owned company, as long as revenue stays under the threshold. For early-stage businesses, freelancers scaling into a company, or small teams billing clients abroad, this often means the corporate tax line on your P&L is simply zero.

DOES YOUR BUSINESS QUALIFY?

The Threshold Is Based on Revenue, Not on Profit or Industry.

What counts against the 14,000,000 ALL threshold is gross annual revenue — total invoiced turnover, not net profit. A consulting business billing €120,000 a year with high margins and one billing the same amount with thin margins are treated the same way for this purpose. There's no separate list of eligible industries: the rule applies broadly across sectors, from digital services and consulting to trading and light manufacturing.

This is also why timing your company registration correctly matters. We review your projected revenue for the year and confirm exactly where you stand relative to the threshold before you register — so there are no surprises when your first tax filing comes due.

GROWING BEYOND THE THRESHOLD

Outgrowing the 0% Rate Isn't a Cliff Edge.

Once annual gross revenue exceeds 14,000,000 ALL, the company moves onto Albania's standard corporate income tax rate — still competitive compared to most of Western Europe, and applied only going forward rather than retroactively on past income. Most founders we work with see this transition as a milestone, not a penalty: it means the business has scaled past early-stage revenue.

Separately, dividends distributed to shareholders are subject to their own withholding tax, regardless of which corporate tax rate applies to the company itself — this is a distinct line item worth planning around from the start, particularly if you intend to pay yourself dividends rather than a salary.
